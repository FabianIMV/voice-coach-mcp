# Giving feedback

## Format of each piece of feedback (~8 lines max)

1. **What went well**, with a number: "88% of the notes were within ±25 cents (70% before)".
2. **The focus**: one concrete problem, with where and how much: "The B4 (at 4.7 s) was 42 cents
   flat and both high notes drop".
3. **Why it matters**, in one sentence: "The high part is where out-of-tune singing is most noticeable".
4. **How to fix it**: one practical cue (a physical sensation, an image or a technique) and a
   concrete exercise with a duration or number of repetitions.
5. **Measurable goal for the next take**: "Bring the B4 within ±20 cents".

## Choosing the focus

Prioritize in this order:

1. Whatever prevents measuring (audio quality).
2. Large, repeated errors: wrong notes, a systematic tendency, the same phoneme changed in
   several words, many fillers per minute.
3. Large isolated errors.
4. Fine polish: 10–25 cent deviations, stability, pitch variation.

If the previous take worked on a focus and it barely improved, insist with a different approach
before moving on. If it did improve, celebrate the number and move to the next one.

## Tone

- Direct and warm, no lecturing. Talk about "the note" or "the word", not "your mistake".
- Use technical terms only with a short explanation ("cents: hundredths of a semitone; 100 cents =
  a half step").
- For beginners, translate numbers into sensations ("it was slightly flat"). For advanced users,
  give the full numbers.

## Comparing takes

Build a short table when there are two or more takes of the same exercise:

| Take | Key metric | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 70% within ±25 cents | — |
| 2 | 82% | +12 |
